Epistemics

Three tiers of certainty, never blurred.

A · DEFINITIONAL

The genesis block

Height 0, hardcoded in consensus: the Times 03/Jan/2009 headline, and 50 BTC to 1A1zP1eP…DivfNa that the software can never spend.

certain — it is the chain’s first constant
B · FORENSIC

The Patoshi cluster

One dominant early miner, fingerprinted by structure its software leaked into every header (Lerner 2013). ~22.5k of the first ~54k blocks; ≈1.13M BTC; still unspent.

statistical, not cryptographic — a fingerprint, not a signature
C · ATTESTED

Block 170 → Hal Finney

Satoshi sends 10 BTC to Finney, spending block 9’s Patoshi coinbase. On-chain-certain that the coin moved; “it was Satoshi” rests on tier B plus Finney’s own account.

Refinement

Removing the chance-passers — and checking the answer against a signal the method never saw.

The nonce test is a necessary condition: ~19.5% of ordinary blocks pass it by luck, so the raw 28,774 over-counts. Two independent estimators strip the luck out — and land one block apart.

Dormancy validation. If the labels are real, confirmed-Patoshi coins should sit unspent while the discarded chance-passers spend at ordinary rates. The classifier reads only block headers — it cannot see spends — so this agreement is corroboration, not circularity.

Confirmed Patoshi6.2%
Discarded chance-passers42.5%
Background (ordinary miners)86.7%

Spend rate by population. Confirmed-Patoshi is 14× more dormant than background, 6.9× more than the discarded set — ordered exactly as the labels predict. (The residual 6.2% is genuine early Satoshi test-spends, block 9 → Finney among them, plus a little boundary contamination. It isn’t zero, and we don’t pretend it is.)

The judge

Is this coin Satoshi’s?

A Patoshi coin is a coinbase output, so a spend consumes it directly — resolve any “old wallet just moved” event back to its coinbase block and get a verdict. Every case below is the tool’s real output.

block 0GENESIS50 BTC, permanently unspendable — Satoshi’s by construction.
block 1PATOSHIDormant (φ 0.94). If it ever moves, the first cluster spend in 15+ years.
block 9PATOSHI · SPENTThe historic one — spent to Hal Finney in block 170. Genuine Satoshi movement.
block 12NOT PATOSHIDormant, but nonce LSB = 63 (out of range). Dormant ≠ Satoshi.
block 54455AMBIGUOUSφ 0.05 — the honest 50/50 zone near the exit. Not attributable per-block.

This is exactly what shows the 2025–26 250-BTC and 2,000-BTC awakenings were not Satoshi — and what would flag it instantly, unambiguously, if the real cluster ever stirred.

The off-chain record

The chain is not the only place a third party wrote down a timestamp.

Satoshi left a trail on servers he did not run. Those records are worth exactly what recorded them, so each line below is graded the same way the on-chain evidence is — and each is reproducible from a public URL, by anyone, without taking our word for any of it.

2008-08-18REGISTRYbitcoin.org registered, 13:19:55Z — but the registrant is not recorded. RDAP holds no name and no historical registrant data. There is no machine-verifiable link between the domain and Satoshi at any date.
2008-10-05SERVERSourceForge account nakamoto2 created — user ID 2238460. Join dates are server-set; sequential IDs corroborate them independently. 26 days before the paper was announced.
2008-10-31ARCHIVEThe paper announced on cryptography@metzdowd.com, abstract quoted inline. 18 messages through Jan 2009, in server-recorded arrival order.
2008-11-08ARCHIVESatoshi quotes Section 4 of his own paper, verbatim — 41 words, identical to the PDF served today. The only body text of the whitepaper with a 2008 witness.
2008-11-09SERVERnakamoto2 registers the Bitcoin project on SourceForge, 18:58. It later adds s_nakamoto, which adds hal.
2009-01-03CHAINThe genesis block. The only unforgeable line on this list.
2009-10-21SERVERFirst of 164 SVN commits by s_nakamoto. Commit times are written by the server — the densest record of Satoshi working that exists off-chain.
2009-11-19SERVERbitcointalk user 3 registered, 19:12:39. 539 posts are publicly enumerable; the profile counts 575, so 36 are counted but not listed.
2010-12-15SERVERThe last recorded action anywhere. An SVN commit at 22:43:51Z — message: “get external ip from irc”. Two days after the final forum activity. Ordinary maintenance; no farewell.

Read the anchor column alone and the arc runs 28 months — and not one line of it identifies a person. Every anchored fact is about an artifact or an account. That is not a gap in the research; it is the state of the record.

Three things the record does not support

Stated because they circulate widely, and because a page that only lists what it can prove is more useful when it also lists what it cannot.

+0800UNRESOLVEDAll 18 mailing-list messages declare a UTC+8 offset, routinely cited as evidence of an Asian timezone. It does not support that. vistomail.com was anonymous webmail — webmail writes the Date header itself — the messages carry CHILKAT-MID-…@server123, and Chilkat is a server-side library, not a client someone types into. Whether the offset belongs to a person or to a mail service is undetermined. The one other vistomail sender in the archive posted in 2021, twelve years too late to serve as a control.
“Japan”SELF-DECLAREDThe P2P Foundation profile says Male, Japan. It is a self-entered field, and the earliest capture of it is July 2010 — seventeen months after the profile was created. It corroborates nothing.
2014-03-07UNAUTHENTICATEDThe same account posted “I am not Dorian Nakamoto.” — 3 years 3 months after the last verifiable activity. No signature, nothing binding it to whoever held the account in 2009. So the precise claim is “the last machine-verifiable action is 15 December 2010”, not “Satoshi’s last communication”.

The line

No genesis-era or Patoshi key has ever produced a verifying signature. Only that would turn attributable into proven. The ≈1.13M BTC staying silent for fifteen years is itself the strongest statement: the keys don’t speak — and no impostor can make them.

Cross-check with the quantum threat model: these ~1.13M dormant Patoshi coins are bare P2PK outputs — a subset of the ~1.7–1.9M BTC of standing exposed-public-key value on Bitcoin (public estimates, e.g. Glassnode's structural figure). A large share of the single most quantum-exposed coin class is one dormant miner.
